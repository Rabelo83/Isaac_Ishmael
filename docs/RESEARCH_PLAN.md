# Research workstreams and source leads

## Research sequence

The following are questions and acquisition targets, not established findings. Research direct authorities and source provenance before drafting conclusions or assigning comparisons legal weight.

| Workstream | Questions | Required outputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genealogies and textual traditions | What do the relevant texts say about Abraham, Isaac, Ishmael, and descendants? When and how are those traditions attested? What is independently corroborated? | Cited textual comparison, source-specific family relationships, dating limits |
| Earliest evidence and ancient history | What can archaeology, inscriptions, and critical scholarship establish about populations and polities? | Timeline with uncertainty and evidence dossiers |
| Successive administrations | How did institutions, populations, and control change through imperial, medieval, Ottoman, and later periods? | Period dossiers and properly sourced maps |
| Modern political and territorial history | What documents and events shaped competing national claims, migration, landholding, displacement, and governance? | Chronology, document register, bounded claims |
| Direct legal authorities | Which instruments and decisions address each distinct right and territory? | Authority register, precise holdings, scope and subsequent status |
| Comparative cases | Which disputes illuminate a specific legal question, and where do comparisons fail? | Inclusion log and comparison dossiers |
| Proposals and negotiations | What was proposed, by whom, and how did named actors respond? | Versioned proposal records and comparison tables |

## Historical coverage checklist

Earliest evidence; ancient Israel and Judah and neighboring societies; successive imperial periods; Roman and Byzantine periods; early Islamic and subsequent administrations; Crusader and Mamluk periods; Ottoman rule; late Ottoman national movements and migrations; British administration and Mandate; partition proposals; 1948 and its consequences; subsequent wars and armistice arrangements; 1967 and its consequences; peace treaties and negotiations; Palestinian and Israeli political developments; events through each release's stated cutoff.

These are organizational topics. Dates, causal explanations, demographic claims, and period boundaries must be sourced during research. Include populations whose experiences do not fit a two-party narrative.

## Legal research acquisition queue

First locate and inspect relevant versions of the UN Charter, Mandate instruments, pertinent General Assembly and Security Council resolutions, armistice agreements, peace treaties, occupation-law instruments, and directly relevant court decisions. Record legal status individually; membership in this queue does not imply equivalent authority or a settled application.

Review the 2004 and 2024 ICJ advisory proceedings and check for subsequent relevant proceedings and instruments through the publication cutoff. Maintain a separate track for individual property and displacement remedies; do not treat every judicial proceeding involving the conflict as a ruling on territorial title.

Initial comparative screening candidates include Western Sahara, Burkina Faso/Mali, Namibia, Island of Palmas, and relevant property/remedy litigation concerning Cyprus. These are leads, not endorsed analogies. Retrieve primary texts, define the narrow comparison question, and explicitly retain or reject each candidate. Add negotiated-settlement comparisons only in a separately labeled category.

## Proposals and parties

Build an inventory spanning early partition proposals, UN proposals, bilateral and multilateral negotiations, peace agreements, autonomy and interim arrangements, regional initiatives, mediator frameworks, and contemporary settlement models. Distinguish a final-status settlement from a ceasefire, interim agreement, or normalization agreement. Exact proposal dates and versions require verification.

Record governments, political organizations, mediators, and affected communities separately, with their roles and dates. No actor automatically represents every member of a population. Track differences within Israeli and Palestinian politics and relevant refugee, minority, and diaspora perspectives when documented.

Every proposal dossier must record:

- Proposer, participants, mediator, date, version, document status, and original links.
- Borders, sovereignty, recognition, security, Jerusalem and holy sites, settlements, refugees, property/restitution or compensation, citizenship and equality, resources, economic arrangements, and implementation guarantees where addressed.
- Named actors' dated responses, conditions, reservations, later revisions, and supporting sources.
- Signature, ratification, adoption, implementation, suspension, or abandonment as distinct states.
- Documented outcomes and competing explanations of failure or success.

Silence is not rejection. An unsigned draft is not an agreement. A participant's memoir is not the agreed text. Distinguish contemporary responses from retrospective accounts; leave an item unknown when the evidence is missing.

## First research sample

Prepare three compact dossiers: one genealogical/textual question, one directly relevant legal authority, and one documented proposal. Each must have inspected sources, precise citations, counterevidence or limitations, and equivalent English and Spanish summaries. Use these to test the editorial method and the proposed website data model before expanding the corpus.
